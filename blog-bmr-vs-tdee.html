BMR vs. TDEE: What's the Difference?

6 min read

Two numbers, one confusion

Every result on this calculator boils down to two numbers: BMR and TDEE. They sound similar, they're calculated from the same inputs, and they get used interchangeably online — which is exactly why so many people end up eating the wrong number of calories.

Get the distinction straight once, and every calorie target you see anywhere will make sense.

BMR: calories at total rest

Your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the energy your body burns just to stay alive — breathing, circulating blood, keeping your organs and brain running — if you did nothing but lie in bed for 24 hours. No walking, no digesting food, no getting up to make coffee.

We estimate it with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation:

Men: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5
Women: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161

BMR is never a number you should eat at for long — it doesn't account for any movement at all, including the incidental movement of a normal day.

TDEE: calories in a real day

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) takes your BMR and multiplies it by an activity factor — a rough estimate of how much extra energy your actual lifestyle burns on top of resting metabolism.

  • Sedentary — desk job, little to no exercise.
  • Lightly active — light exercise 1-3 days a week.
  • Moderately active — moderate exercise 3-5 days a week.
  • Very active — hard exercise 6-7 days a week.
  • Extra active — hard daily exercise plus a physically demanding job.

TDEE is the number that actually reflects your day-to-day life, which is why it's the baseline every calorie target — lose, maintain, or gain — is built from.
